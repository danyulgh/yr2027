> rhetorical situation

# exigence
* an imperfection marked by an urgency
* features
	* perceived well or not
	* real or unreal
	* important or trivial
	* easily modifiable or not
* questions:
	* how do you characterize the exigence?
	* to what extent is exigence shared between the audience and writer/speaker?

# audience
* a rhetorical audience consists only of those persons who are capable of being influenced by discourse (language) and of being mediators of change (those who can actually make change)
* can be real, imagined, or evoked
* questions:
	* what does the audience already know about the subject?
	* how closely aligned are the writer and audience?

# constraints
* every rhetorical situation contains a set of constraints made up of persons, events, objects, and relations which are parts of the situation because they have the power to constrain decision and action neeeded to modify the exigence
* standard sources of contraints

# appeals
* ethos - refers to writer's credibility/authority
	* situated: derived from position, expertise, etc.
	* invented: created within the text
	* varies by culture and community
	* methods:
		* recalling history of action or deeds
		* establishing understanding with audience by claiming similarities in experiences or beliefs
		* claiming expertise via education or research
* pathos - emotional state of the audience
	* audience's feelings help frame
		* understanding of the argument
		* whether audience can be persuaded
	* goal: bring pathos into alignment with your arguments/conclusions
	* pathos can be induced or alleviated by the text, directly or indirectly (e.g., metaphor)
